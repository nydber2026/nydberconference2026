Upstate NYDBER 2026 brought together Discipline-Based Education Research (DBER) scholars — across Physics (PER), Biology (BER), Engineering (EER), Chemistry (CER), Geoscience (GER), and related fields — from across Upstate New York for two days at Cornell University. The conference was designed as a three-phase consensus-building process.

Before
Pre-Conference Survey
53 responses (41 completed). Hopes for the conference; ranked barriers & enablers to InterDBER collaboration.
During
Working Group Documents
9 small groups, both days. Naming specific barriers, bright spots, design principles, and draft InterDBER research questions.
After
Post-Conference Survey
21 responses (19 completed). Conference evaluation, concrete follow-up plans, and 3-month commitments.
About this report: Barriers and enablers below are kept close to each working group's original language on purpose — this is meant to become the input for the next step in the consensus process: a community-wide rating of each barrier's severity and changeability (and each enabler's potential and feasibility), which has not yet been conducted. All quotes and group notes are anonymized to theme- or group-level attribution; no individual names appear in this report.
01 · Participants

Who Was in the Room

Nine small groups met throughout the conference, each spanning multiple institutions, DBER fields, and career stages — from graduate students to senior faculty, across R1 universities, primarily-undergraduate institutions, and community colleges.

02 · Pre-Conference

What People Came Hoping For

Common threads: finding collaborators with complementary skills or shared interests; learning how other DBER fields approach shared problems (assessment, AI in the classroom, retention/belonging); and bringing something concrete to the table — a dataset, methods expertise, or lived experience navigating a specific institutional barrier.

05 · Consensus Building

What Blocks Collaboration

Pre-conference: ranked barriers

RankSample responses (InterDBER-field barriers)
1st (hardest)
  • Establishing networks/lack of awareness of other DBER research, especially if someone's student and post-doc…
  • DBER labs or programs are often siloed (or even the only ones at their institution)
  • Lack of common research interests
  • field specific language
2nd
  • Differences in research trends, as the topic of interest may vary across different DBER fields
  • History of separation to overcome
  • Lack of institutional support for initiatives of cross-collaboration
  • different state of the art
3rd (easiest)
  • Discipline-blind methods designs are difficult to conceive
  • Different educational priorities within each field
  • Lack of knowledge about people willing to collaborate
  • Differing P&T/graduation requirements that have different incentive structures

08 · Evaluation

Conference Feedback

Overall experience

Component ratings

What people said was most valuable

  • Becoming aware of upstate NY institutions with DBER groups and some of the ways in which these groups are internally organized/supported (CASTLE and CDER) and making some initial connections with people in these groups.
  • Networking. It was good to connect with people who are working in DBER across fields and see where we have shared interests and concerns.
  • Getting feedback on work; Connecting with other DBER faculty at RIT
  • I liked the poster sessions the most, and I felt that gave me the best opportunity to learn about others' research.
  • Collecting together people who may otherwise not be able to attend a research conference in one place, and connecting them with folks whose primary role is research.
  • meeting people from across the region and across fields
  • Informal chatting time
  • Making friends and sharing research in the poster session.

What could have improved the experience

  • The underlying goal seemed to be about trying to establish collaborations between DBER groups or between disciplines. But—for those of us outside of the already-established groups—I don't think we're yet in a position…
  • I think having more time to talk about ongoing projects in a more structured way–birds of a feather discussions, or project ideation for collaborations would have been nice.
  • I would have been interested in some talks/panel sessions. The content grouping of the posters meant that people could not see each other's work as well, but if there we 10 minute talks, we could each hear more from…
  • While I found the working groups valuable, I would have preferred more opportunities to hear more deeply about other DBER research (e.g., in poster sessions or, perhaps more preferably in talks). Perhaps this sounds…
  • I would have enjoyed a wider variety of activities---it felt like there was a lot of effort put on participants to make the conference valuable by continuing to have thoughtful conversation throughout the day. Maybe…
  • not staying with the same group as much for working sessions
  • More space and breaks for the introverts <3
  • Maybe spacing the first day out a bit more. Also having some opportunities to go on walks/hikes with the visitors and doing informal active activities with them.
